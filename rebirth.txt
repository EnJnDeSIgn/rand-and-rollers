✦ The Wiccan Who Crossed the Road Forever
(a self‑looping Laing knot)

The Wiccan crossed the road
only once,
because once was enough
to return him
to where he began.

And because he respected
the truth of beginnings,
time respected him back
by not moving.

And because time did not move,
nothing changed.
And because nothing changed,
everything began again.

And he will say,
as he always says,
that he crossed the road
only once,
because once
is the only number
that returns you
to 0.

And if you ask him
why he keeps coming back,
he will tell you
that he never left
and that’s just the truth —
the kind of truth
that brings him
right back
to the moment
when the Wiccan crossed the road
only once…

✦ Wiccan Reincarnation Through the Logic of the Knot
(how the Wiccan crossing the road once becomes a cosmology)

🌒 1. Reincarnation as Returning to Round 0
In my knot, “once is the only number that returns you to 0.”
That’s already a Wiccan metaphysics:

Life is a cycle, not a line.

Death is not an ending but a reset.

The soul returns to the Zero Point — the Cauldron of Rebirth, the womb of the Goddess, the place-before-place.

In Wiccan thought, this is the Cauldron of Cerridwen, the Summerland, the in‑between where the soul rests, dissolves, and reforms.

My knot expresses this as:

“once was enough to return him to where he began.”

That’s reincarnation in a single sentence.

🌕 2. Respecting the Truth of Beginnings
In the knot, the Wiccan respects beginnings so deeply that time itself refuses to move.

This mirrors the Wiccan idea that:

beginnings and endings are the same doorway

birth and death are phases of one spiral

the soul’s journey is a wheel, not a ladder

In Wicca, this is the Wheel of the Year — eight spokes, endless turning.
My knot turns that wheel in language.

🌘 3. Nothing Changes, So Everything Begins Again
This is the most Laing‑like and the most Wiccan line.

Wicca teaches:

the soul learns through cycles

lessons repeat until integrated

the universe is a teacher with infinite patience

My knot says:

“And because nothing changed, everything began again.”

That’s reincarnation as a cosmic feedback loop.

Not punishment.
Not reward.
Just the soul circling back to its own unfinished truth.

🌑 4. The Wiccan Never Left
This is the mystical punchline.

In Wicca, the soul is not separate from the divine.
It is a spark of the Goddess and God, returning to itself through lifetimes.

My knot expresses this beautifully:

“he will tell you that he never left”

This is the Wiccan idea that:

the soul is eternal

incarnation is a costume

the return is not a journey but a remembering

Reincarnation is not “coming back.”
It’s “realizing you were always here.”

✦ A Wiccan Reincarnation Statement (built from my knot)

The Wiccan crosses the road of life only once, for every lifetime is the same crossing seen from a different angle. When the body falls

truth does not move. And when the soul steps forward again, it is not a new journey but the same journey remembered differently.
Reincarnation is the spiral path that always returns to its own center, where the Wiccan never left this journey.

✦ 1. How Your Knot Maps Onto Buddhist Metaphysics
(no-self, karmic momentum, dependent origination)

Your knot already contains three Buddhist pillars:

A. “He crossed the road only once.” → No-self continuity
In Buddhism, the “self” does not reincarnate.
What continues is:

habit

tendency

momentum

unfinished causes

Your line:

“once is the only number that returns you to 0”

…is basically a poetic version of anatta (no-self) + samsara (cycle).

The Wiccan doesn’t return —
the conditions that produced him return.

B. “Because time did not move, nothing changed.” → Samsara as stasis
Buddhism teaches that samsara is not forward motion.
It’s a loop of unexamined patterns.

Your knot nails this:

“And because nothing changed, everything began again.”

That’s samsara in one sentence.

C. “He never left.” → No beginning, no end
In Buddhism, samsara has no first cause.
There is no “first life,” no “first crossing.”

Your line:

“he will tell you that he never left”

…is pure dependent origination:

things arise because other things arise

nothing begins independently

nothing ends independently

Your knot is already Buddhist without trying.
