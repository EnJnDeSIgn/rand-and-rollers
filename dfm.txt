The Devil » The Fool = The Magician
(a life rewritten in metaphor)

He was born in a house of drifting rooms,
where memory slipped through the floorboards
before he could learn its name.
Every corridor turned itself around,
and every face became a mask
he had to trust without knowing why.

In that wandering childhood,
he walked as the Fool walks—
open‑handed, open‑hearted,
easy to mislead,
but impossible to break.

One day, on the road of uncounted steps,
he met a stranger who carried a single coin—
a small sun,
a quiet riddle,
a circle that asked to be learned.
He turned it in his palm
until the turning turned him,
until the metal remembered him
better than he remembered himself.

He practiced in secret,
as those who are underestimated often do,
and the coin obeyed—
vanishing, returning,
teaching him the grammar of impossible things.
The Fool became the Magician
not by being taught,
but by refusing to stop learning.

There had been a wound once—
sharp, sudden,
the kind that splits a life
into Before and After.
But even wounds have teachers.
From that opening
came a breath‑art,
a quiet discipline
he had forgotten he knew.

A gentle friend
placed the old practice back in his hands,
and though others doubted,
he followed the thread anyway—
through pages,
through silence,
through the long corridor of his own mind.

Dreams returned first,
then memories,
then the shape of the self
that had been waiting behind the door.

Drums called him next—
a circle of hands,
a rhythm of remembering.
Their music shook loose
what had been buried,
what had been stolen,
what had been sleeping.

And somewhere in that rhythm
stood a teacher made of shadow and steel—
harsh, imperfect,
yet part of the architecture
that shaped his early strength.
Even the difficult figures
leave their fingerprints
on the doorway of becoming.

Now he stands
not as the child of forgetting,
nor the Fool of wandering roads,
but as the Magician—
the one who learned
to turn wounds into windows,
coins into constellations,
and memory into a craft
that no one can take from him.
