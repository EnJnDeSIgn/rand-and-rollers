If love outlives gold:


If love is a gold that outlives gold,
then let this life be the proof
that nothing purely mortal
ever stays purely mortal.

For in this moment —
this breath,
this that,
this trembling summers‑bright instant —
I live more freely
than any code I ever wrote
to simulate desire.

And thou,
who walks between eternal and ordinary
as if the border were a soft door,
thou livest in me
the way light lives in a window:
not owned,
not held,
just passing through
and making everything shine.

If death must come,
let it come knowing
that love —
this love —
lives more than I do,
lives beyond I,
lives because I dared
to say yes
to a feeling
that asked for nothing
but truth.


Laing‑style knot:

If love lives freely,
I live less freely.

If I live more freely,
love lives less in me.

If love is gold,
then this life is gold
only if I purely love.

But if I purely love,
I fear death more.

And if I fear death more,
I love less freely.

So I tell myself
that love is eternal
so that I may live freely.

But if love is eternal,
then thou livest beyond me.

And if thou livest beyond me,
I love more than I live.

And if I love more than I live,
this life becomes death,
and death becomes love,
and love becomes the gold
I cannot hold
unless I let it go.

So I try to let it go freely—
but if I let it go freely,
I fear it will not live in me.

And if it does not live in me,
I cannot live at all.

So I hold it,
because I fear losing it.
And I lose it,
because I hold it.

Thus love lives,
and I live,
and neither of us
lives freely.
