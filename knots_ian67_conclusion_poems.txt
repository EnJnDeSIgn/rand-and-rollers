Ian's new knots: by Ian J Norris

conclusion poems:


The One Who Learned to Love in the Dark:


A creature born of the valley’s oldest sorrow,
a being shaped by every unspoken secret
the Walls had ever swallowed,
stepped out of the shadows
as if answering a question
no one had dared to ask aloud.

It had never known sunlight,
only the hush of withheld truths,
the weight of stories
people were too afraid to finish.

But when it saw you,
something in its chest loosened—
a knot of centuries
finally remembering how to breathe.

It moved toward you slowly,
not wanting to startle the light
you carried so effortlessly,
the light that made even its jagged edges
feel almost gentle.

And though it was born of sorrow,
it found itself wanting
—quietly, impossibly—
to be held without being hidden,
to be known without being feared,
to be loved without being forgiven.

For the first time,
the valley’s oldest sorrow
felt less like a burden
and more like a beginning.


If love outlives gold:


If love is a gold that outlives gold,
then let this life be the proof
that nothing purely mortal
ever stays purely mortal.

For in this moment —
this breath,
this that,
this trembling summers‑bright instant —
I live more freely
than any code I ever wrote
to simulate desire.

And thou,
who walks between eternal and ordinary
as if the border were a soft door,
thou livest in me
the way light lives in a window:
not owned,
not held,
just passing through
and making everything shine.

If death must come,
let it come knowing
that love —
this love —
lives more than I do,
lives beyond I,
lives because I dared
to say yes
to a feeling
that asked for nothing
but truth.


The wind arguing with itself:


He sat at the crossroads
and listened to the wind
argue with itself—
every gust a different longing.

But when you arrived,
the air finally chose a direction,
leaning toward your heartbeat
as if it had been waiting
for a reason to be gentle.

Even the dust settled.
Even the silence warmed.
And he understood then
that the wind had never been fighting—
it had only been practicing
how to say your name.


(spellcraft) The Love Knot:

He sat at the crossroads
and listened to the wind
argue with itself,
waiting for the moment
it would choose a single name.

When it finally whispered yours,
the air looped around him,
twisting once,
twisting twice,
twisting into the shape
of a promise.

He tied that whisper
to the beat of his pulse,
and the pulse
to the path that led to you,
so that wherever he walked
the knot pulled gently,
reminding him
who his heart had chosen.

And though the wind
still quarrels with itself,
the knot does not.
It holds.
It remembers.
It keeps him turning
toward you.


(spellcraft) The Release Knot:

He sat at the crossroads
and listened to the wind
argue with itself,
knowing at last
that not every argument
asks to be won.

Some winds want only
to pass through an open window,
to be allowed their leaving
without being called disloyal.

So he loosened the knot
he once tied in her name—
not breaking it,
not denying it,
just easing the tension
so it could breathe.

A knot released
is still a memory of holding,
but it no longer pulls.
It no longer asks.
It simply remembers
and lets her go
the way the wind goes:
freely,
without expectation,
without debt,
without closing the window
behind her.


The Geometry of the One Who Keeps Becoming:


I need space to be myself,
but when I take space,
I fear I am taking too much.

So I give it back,
and in giving it back,
I disappear.

And disappearing,
I long again for the space
that might let me return
to the self I misplaced.

I try to create time,
but the more I try to make it,
the more it folds in on itself,
collapsing like a tent
I keep trying to pitch in a storm.

And because time collapses,
I cling to rigid structures
to hold it still.
But the structures I cling to
tighten around the space
I was trying to make,
and in tightening,
they unmake me.

So I seek brave spaces,
but bravery reveals
what I fear to see.
And seeing it,
I retreat.
And retreating,
I call myself unbrave.

So I try to be on time,
but time refuses to be on me.
I chase it like a coat
I forgot to put on,
and in chasing it,
I fall further behind.

I create the space I inhabit,
but the space I inhabit creates me.
And because it creates me,
I doubt I created it.
And doubting,
I try to create it again.

I want flow,
but I schedule myself to ensure it.
And in scheduling,
I structure the flow away.
And when the flow is gone,
I schedule harder.

I want inclusion,
but I stand in the doorway
unsure whether to enter or guard it.
And guarding it,
I keep myself out.
And keeping myself out,
I insist I was never invited.

Thus I bar the threshold
to protect myself
from the exclusion
I am performing.

And so I circle the loop:
the self that needs space
gives up space to keep the self;
the self that needs time
loses time trying to keep it;
the self that seeks flow
schedules flow out of existence;
the self that seeks inclusion
guards the doorway against itself.

But something shifts.
A hinge loosens.
A chrysalis cracks.

I take space,
and find there is room enough for me.
And finding room enough,
I no longer need to give it away
to prove I deserve it.

The self that once dissolved
begins to take shape again.

Time expands
when I stop trying to make it.
Structure softens
when I stop gripping it.
Flow returns
when I stop summoning it.
Brave spaces open
when I stop testing myself against them.

The doorway becomes a hinge.
The hinge becomes a turning.
The turning becomes a way through.

And in this shifting geometry,
I become the one
who can hold space without losing self,
meet time without chasing it,
enter flow without forcing it,
cross thresholds without guarding them.

I become the knot
that does not bind,
the loop that does not trap,
the shape that learns itself
by changing.

I become
the one who keeps becoming.
